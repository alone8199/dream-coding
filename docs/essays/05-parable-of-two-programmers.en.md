# 5. Parable of Two Programmers and a Morning That Did Not Wake

[中文](./05-parable-of-two-programmers.md) | [English](./05-parable-of-two-programmers.en.md)

Once upon a time there were two programmers, one named Bai (White) and one named Ye (Night).

Bai believed in daytime. Every morning at seven he got up, opened his editor in the sunlight, wrote code line by line and fixed bugs one by one. His code was tidy and clear, and every commit came with careful notes. Everyone said he was a reliable man.

Ye believed in night. Every evening he closed his eyes and wrote code in his dreams. His dreams were long and vast, with no edges. When he woke, he could see only a single segfault and some hazy pictures. He often said, "I've finished writing it. I just haven't fully remembered it yet."

One day the two of them ran into the same problem. Bai spent a whole day finding the cause and fixed it. Ye spent a whole night dreaming up an answer, but when he woke he remembered only a single color.

Bai asked Ye, "Did you actually solve the problem?"

Ye thought for a long time, then said, "I don't know. But I know that in the dream, the problem never existed."

Bai was stunned. He wanted to say that a problem is a problem, and should not vanish just because of a dream. But then he remembered how many times he had fixed a bug without knowing why it had been fixed.

Later, they went together to watch the sunrise. Bai looked at the sun, and Ye looked at the sun's shadow. Neither of them spoke.

The moral of the story does not lie in who was right and who was wrong. It only reminds us that daylight lets us be certain, and night lets us forget. Forgetting and certainty are both part of writing code.
