# Interpreting Dreams: Recording What Remains After Waking

[中文](./09-interpreting-dreams.md) | [English](./09-interpreting-dreams.en.md)

After waking, dream coding enters the stage that requires the most patience: dream interpretation. The goal of interpretation is not to fix errors, but to extract useful information from the residual impressions. These impressions are usually vague: sometimes only an image, a sentence, or an indescribable feeling is left. Interpretation means recording them as fully as possible and judging whether they are worth keeping.

The first step of interpretation is to record while the memory is still warm. Memories of dreams fade quickly, and the first few minutes after waking are when details are easiest to recall. It is recommended that you do not check your phone or email right after waking. Instead, pick up a pen and paper or open a blank document, and write down everything you remember, even if it is only a few words.

The second step is to separate images from logic. Content in dreams is often pictorial: a door, a bridge, an exam. These images do not necessarily correspond to any structure in the code, but they may hint at a relationship. For example, dreaming of walking the same staircase may mean something has gone wrong with a loop or a recursion, and dreaming of being unable to find something may mean a variable was not defined correctly. The interpreter needs to translate the images into hypotheses that can be tested.

The third step is to compare the record with the script. Put what you recorded and last night's dream script side by side, and see which parts appeared in the dream and which were ignored. This comparison often reveals problems the developer did not notice during the day, such as a step that was skipped or a condition that was too loose.

The fourth step is to decide whether to adopt the result. The results of interpretation do not have to be applied immediately. Some insights can be verified during the day, and some are just interesting clues that are worth recording but not acting on yet. Dream coding encourages the developer to stay skeptical before adopting anything, because the interpretation of a dream may itself be a projection.

The product of interpretation can be a short note. It may include a summary of the dream, the images you consider important, the hypotheses they suggest, and the next step for verification. The note does not need to be complete. Its value lies in letting you quickly return to that night's thinking when you look back during the day.

It should be noted that dream interpretation is not divination. Dream coding does not hold that dreams can give answers directly, only that they offer a new angle on the problem. The quality of an interpretation depends on the developer's judgment, not on how mysterious the dream is.

In a team, interpretation can also become a shared ritual. Once a week, everyone tells about their recent dreams, and the group discusses the parts that seem valuable. Such discussions often spark new ideas and also build a shared language among practitioners of dream coding.
